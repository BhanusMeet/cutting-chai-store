Four Generations at the Counter


In the spring of 1912, Josiah Holloway hung a green shingle over a narrow shop on Mill Street and began compounding remedies for his neighbours. Back then a prescription was a conversation, and a good druggist knew every customer by name.

His sons, Edmund and Walter, kept the lamps lit through two wars and a depression, and the recipe book passed to their children, and then to theirs. The mortar and pestle on our counter is the same one Josiah used.

Today we still make our tonics and salves in small batches, from honest ingredients, and we still like to know your name.
